# Dubai Luxury Villa AI — v0.3 Art Direction

## North star

**Quiet Luxury + Resort Hospitality + Modern Desert Elegance**

The v0.3 direction intentionally moves away from visual excess. The villa should read as a calm, private, high-end residence whose luxury comes from proportion, material restraint, light, indoor/outdoor continuity, and a coherent experience.

## Research signals

The direction is informed by current high-end residential and architectural-visualization references:

- Gensler residential work emphasizes hospitality-driven living, natural accents such as wood/stone/concrete, abundant daylight, views, wellness, and sanctuary-like experience.
- Gensler Solhouse 6035 explicitly uses “outside-in” living, retractable glazing, terraces, green space, and wellness amenities.
- Perkins&Will’s Jeddah Shangri-La uses context, climate, culture, generous exterior spaces, privacy, and sun/wind response rather than a generic all-glass luxury language.
- Autodesk’s architectural-rendering guidance treats accurate design intent plus light, shadow, reflection, landscape, and context as core to exterior visualization.
- Chaos Veras 4.0 emphasizes design-faithful geometry, material consistency, reduced visual errors, and consistent multi-angle output rather than AI-generated drift.

## Production order

**Form → Material → Light → Life → Presentation**

Do not try to rescue weak architecture with a dramatic sunset. Each stage has its own approval gate.

## v0.3-r1 — Form gate

Goal: a premium silhouette in a neutral clay render before material or lifestyle polish.

### P0 changes

- Reduce the composition to a few confident architectural volumes.
- Make the ground floor read as an open pavilion facing the pool.
- Make the upper floor lighter and deliberately asymmetric.
- Keep one strong stone spine as a structural anchor.
- Deepen the terrace so it reads as an outdoor room.
- Use one clear roof/cantilever gesture rather than several competing gestures.
- Lengthen and simplify the pool axis.
- Keep the camera near human eye level with a restrained architectural lens.
- Remove furniture, vegetation, decorative lighting, and other lifestyle noise from the clay review.

### r1 acceptance gate

The r1 clay render is acceptable only if all are true:

1. The villa reads as luxury architecture without textures, landscaping, or sunset mood.
2. The main horizontal gesture is obvious but not oversized.
3. The stone spine balances the lighter upper volume.
4. The pool, terrace, and living frontage form one coherent axis.
5. The building looks plausible from a human-height three-quarter view.
6. No decorative object is being used to hide weak massing.

If the clay render fails this gate, iterate the geometry before moving to materials.

## v0.3-r2 — Material gate

Target palette:

- warm limestone / travertine
- soft mineral plaster
- warm walnut / teak-toned timber
- restrained graphite or dark bronze metal
- neutral architectural glass
- calm deep-aqua pool water

Limit the primary architectural palette to roughly four material families plus glass and water. Avoid plastic gloss and random material changes between volumes.

## v0.3-r3 — Light gate

Required lighting studies:

- Golden Hour hero
- Blue Hour prestige
- restrained Night mode

The goal is soft shadow structure, readable glass, warm interior life, and controlled reflections. Bloom and emissive accents must remain subordinate to the architecture.

## v0.3-r4 — Life gate

Reintroduce only curated lifestyle cues:

- one outdoor lounge composition
- a readable living-room furniture group behind glazing
- minimal desert planting
- one or two focal landscape elements
- subtle path and planting lights

Avoid resort clutter, dense random vegetation, and inconsistent furniture styles.

## v0.3-r5 — Presentation gate

Final portfolio set should include:

- hero golden-hour exterior
- blue-hour pool shot
- living-room-to-terrace view
- elevated overview
- interactive GLB viewer
- source `.blend`, validated `.glb`, render receipts, and QA notes

## Claim boundary

This remains an AI-assisted architectural visualization and interactive portfolio prototype. It is **not** BIM, structural engineering, construction documentation, permitting documentation, a valuation, or a sales forecast.
